Method: homepage plus one or two key pages per site. One entry per competitor, committed as soon as it's written. Quotes are short and verbatim; everything else is paraphrase.

## Deep dives (round three)
Several pages per site. The question each time: how does the product describe the PSMF problem, and where does its answer stop?

### Veeva SafetyDocs: the PSMF in depth
Pages: `/modern-pharmacovigilance-system-master-file-management/`, the white paper "How to Globalize PSMF Management…", Vault help on the automatic logbook (Oct 2026)

- **Page promise:** "Rapidly generate an accurate PSMF on-demand." Sections: "Gain Complete Oversight" ("Demonstrate QPPV oversight"), "Role-based Collaboration", "Harmonize Processes". The QPPV is named as the person served, which no other vendor does.
- **The problem, in their words (white paper):** "One of the biggest challenges in maintaining a PSMF is keeping content current." Content comes from departments outside safety, which "causes difficulties identifying content owners and verifying that content is current." "With a manual process, it is challenging to keep the logbook up to date." Production is "labor-intensive and manual."
- **Their ideal state:** one validated global platform; a global main body with local annexes; "virtual binders" that link documents and generate the PSMF in one click; content owners update their own documents; scheduled review reminders based on SOPs and automated emails to owners; the logbook built automatically from logbook entries; e-approval by the QPPV; dashboards of overdue tasks. End state: the PSMF as "an asset" for monitoring the PV system, not a static repository.
- **Numbers (customer-attributed):** a top-10 biopharma cut authoring-to-approval cycle time by 30%; a PSMF steward went from "many hours" to about 10 minutes to publish (the product page's Moderna quote says under 30 minutes).
- **Where it stops:** the unit is the **document**. Owners keep their documents current; Veeva links, reminds, assembles and logs. Nothing checks whether what an owner wrote is right, consistent with the rest of the file, or still mentioned correctly in Section 2 after Annex B changed. The logbook is generated from entries people write.
- **For Veria:** the most important finding of round three. Veeva has already described half of Veria's loop as its own ideal state (owners asked on a schedule, automated emails, automatic logbook, QPPV oversight). So "we remind the owners" is not a differentiator against the market leader. What Veeva doesn't do is work at the level of the **fact**: one fact stored once, every change checked by rules and AI, every mention found and redrafted, a redline against the last signed version. The pitch against Veeva is not "we ask the owners" but "we know what every answer changes". Also: "PSMF as an asset for monitoring the PV system" is a direction line in Veeva's own paper that the product page never uses. Veria could take that ground (the file that tells the QPPV how the system is really running).

### PSMF Manager: in depth
Pages: homepage, `/features/external-data-sources`, `/pricing`, blog post "PSMF Change Tracking From Manual Logs to Automation" (Oct 2026)

- **Product shape:** a document editor with workflow. Revisions go Initiate → Edit → Review → Draft → Approve → Lock. An admin unlocks a draft; users with roles edit it. Vendors get restricted access to the modules or data requests assigned to them. Part 11 e-signatures, version history, tracked changes.
- **AI:** "AI Review Assistant" (remarks on updated content), "Auto-Annex I Generator" (change-log entries generated from updates), "Ask AI". The blog says AI should not approve changes or replace QPPV judgment.
- **Connectors already claimed:** "External Data Sources" links to systems "having webhooks and open APIs" and to document repositories, screening "every 24 hours" by default. A changed source file creates a change request for review; with auto-update on, the file is copied into the PSMF and logged in Annex I. No systems are named, and nothing says incoming data is checked.
- **Pricing shape:** Standard (up to 20 users, data migration included, support in 2 business days) and Enterprise (up to 50 users, private subdomain, isolated database, region choice, 4-day rollback). Plus an unexplained "Multi Tenant for Vendors" plan. No prices shown. 3-month free trial.
- **The problem, in their words (blog):** "Change Log entries added after the update", "Approvals stored in emails", "Annex updates tracked in separate files", "The biggest risk is retrospective reconstruction."
- **Where it stops:** the unit is a document section or a linked file. People edit text; the system tracks who did what. Nothing works out what one change means for the rest of the file, and the connector moves files, not facts.
- **For Veria:** weak as a brand, but it has already put two of Veria's ideas on its feature list: an AI-drafted logbook and syncing from source systems. So "connects to your systems" alone won't land as new. What makes Veria's version different is what happens after the data arrives: checked, matched to the fact it changes, every mention updated, held for a person if anything looks wrong. Their pain list ("approvals stored in emails", "retrospective reconstruction") is a good, specific picture of the "before"; the same pains could carry a visual. The vendor tier is a reminder that PV service providers maintaining PSMFs for several MAHs are a buyer type with their own plan.

### Quartica MARS: PSMF module

## Synthesis
Covers both rounds: 18 entries (Insife was acquired by Qinecsa, so it has no entry of its own).

### What everyone promises
- **"Inspection-ready, always" / "constant audit readiness."** Qinecsa, PSMF Manager, Veeva, ProPharma, Qualio, Greenlight Guru. The category's baseline claim, so it can't be Veria's headline. It can be the result we back up.
- **"Effortless", "efficiency", "burden to advantage."** Extedo, IQVIA, Veeva, MasterControl. Worn out.
- **"AI-first", "agentic", "touchless", "autopilot."** ArisGlobal, Drata, Qualio, Freyr, Tepsivo. "Agentic" is now in nearly every headline, from PV to QMS. Loud, abstract, and it removes the human. Wrong register for a QPPV who signs personally.
- **"One platform, no silos."** Ennov, ArisGlobal, Freyr, Veeva. Every suite claims the whole lifecycle.

### What nobody promises
- **That the file is true.** Everyone sells storage, assembly, staff or readiness. No one says the PSMF matches reality: no contradictions, nothing missing, nothing stale. That's Veria's enemy (mistakes reaching the inspector), and no one else has claimed it.
- **That the work disappears.** Vendors speed the work up. Service providers move it to their team ("Move local compliance from your team to ours"). Nobody in PV says people just do their jobs and the file follows. Greenlight Guru comes closest, in medtech ("without your team manually holding it together").
- **That the person who signs can see everything.** No one speaks to the QPPV's own accountability or visibility.
- **Who approves the AI's work.** Biologit says "no black boxes", others have AI trust centers, but nobody states plainly that a person approves every change. Veria can.

### The feeling everyone is after
"Sleep at night" turns up three times (PrimeVigilance, Qualio, and Vanta's "Cool, calm"), always in a customer's mouth, never the vendor's. It's the emotional core of the market. Veria can say it in its own voice if it's backed up right next to it: nothing gets signed while a check is still open.

### How they prove it
- Big players use scale: logos, decades, customer counts, regulators as users. Not available to us.
- Smaller and service players use people and openness: named experts, published compliance rates, public pricing (Tepsivo), read-only client access, concrete service commitments.
- Adjacent SaaS uses real-looking product UI with specific numbers, a public trust center, and a section on how validation works.
- AI trust centers are becoming standard (ArisGlobal, MasterControl).
- Warning: four sites (PrimeVigilance, Biologit, MasterControl, and Ennov's image tiles) ship stat bars with missing or placeholder numbers. A broken proof is worse than none. Veria has no customer numbers yet, so it shouldn't build a stat bar.
- Veria can use: domain identity ("built by people who've done the job", in the confident form of Greenlight Guru's "Nobody knows medtech like…"), the controls (Annex 11 / Part 11), how validation works, the 0-of-45 test run, the PSMF shown as it was on any date, a trust center page with a plain AI statement.

### Register
Between IQVIA's "expertise plus AI" and Greenlight Guru and Vanta's calm confidence. Human first, concrete, quiet. No jokes, no space metaphors, no "agentic", no "revolution" (Tepsivo shows how the maverick register reads). Biologit is the closest to our own copy rules: short fragments, narrow focus said with pride.

### Narrow focus is a strength
Biologit ("Literature Monitoring Is All We Do") and Greenlight Guru ("Nobody knows medtech like…") make a single focus sound strong, not small, while the suites blur together. Veria should own the PSMF completely and let "bigger than one document" show as growth outward from it, not as a lifecycle-platform claim.

### Direction statement: candidates
The open question in `PRODUCT.md`. Each one claims ground nobody holds and is backed by the engine Veria already has (one fact stored once, every change checked, every mention updated, redline, sign).

1. **"A PSMF that's true every day, not just inspection day."** Takes the "every day, not once a year" arc from compliance SaaS and points it at truth, not readiness.
2. **"People do their jobs. The PSMF follows."** The connectors vision in six words. Human, and the opposite of "touchless".
3. **"Nothing reaches the inspector that you haven't seen."** Built around the enemy and the QPPV's signature. Strongest on trust, weakest on scale.
4. **"Compliance that keeps itself current. Starting with the PSMF."** Carries "bigger than one document" without overcompensating. The widest of the four.
5. **"Spend less time on the PSMF and more on safety."** Adapted from Greenlight Guru. Warm, aimed at the QPPV, but borrowed in shape.

Recommendation: unchanged after round two. 2 as the direction, 1 as the page-level promise, 3 for the signing and trust section. Round two backed this up: "agentic" and "always ready" got more crowded, while "the file is true" and "people, not autopilot" are still unclaimed.
