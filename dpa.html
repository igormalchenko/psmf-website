Data processing agreement

In effect from 19 October 2026

This agreement sets out how we process personal data on behalf of our customers, as Article 28 of the GDPR requires. It is part of every agreement for the Veria service and applies automatically; no separate signature is needed. Customers who want a signed copy can ask us for one.

1. Parties and roles

The Customer (as defined in our terms) is the controller. Igor Malchenko, trading as Veria, ul. Sarmacka 19/138, 02-972 Warsaw, Poland, NIP 9512574134 ("Veria") is the processor. Where the Customer is itself a processor for another company, such as a PV service provider keeping a client's PSMF, Veria is its subprocessor and this agreement applies in the same way.

Words defined in the GDPR, such as "personal data", "processing" and "personal data breach", have the same meaning here.

2. What we process

Subject matter
Providing the Veria service under the terms
Duration
For as long as the agreement runs, and until the data is deleted under section 10
Nature and purpose
Storing, organising, checking, displaying, exporting and deleting the content of the Customer's PSMF and related records; sending emails to Users; producing AI suggestions for people to review; keeping an audit trail and records of signatures
Data subjects
The Customer's employees, QPPVs and deputies, contractors, and the staff of its partners and service providers named in the PSMF; the Customer's Users
Types of data
Names, job titles and roles, business contact details, qualifications and CVs, organisational information, and the records of who did what in the service, such as answers, approvals, signatures, timestamps and IP addresses
Special categories
None. The Customer must not upload health data, individual case safety reports or other special categories of personal data unless agreed in writing.

3. Instructions

We process personal data only on the Customer's documented instructions. The agreement and the Customer's use and settings of the service are those instructions. If the law requires us to process data otherwise, we will tell the Customer first, unless the law forbids this. We will tell the Customer if we believe an instruction breaks data protection law.

4. Confidentiality

Everyone we authorise to process the personal data is bound to keep it confidential.

5. Security

We take the technical and organisational measures required by Article 32 GDPR, listed in the annex below. We may improve them over time, but never lower the overall level of protection.

6. Subprocessors

The Customer gives us general authorisation to use subprocessors. Our current subprocessors are listed on our subprocessors page. We will email the Customer at least 30 days before adding or replacing one. If the Customer objects on reasonable data protection grounds, we will discuss the objection in good faith; if we cannot resolve it, the Customer may terminate the affected service and receive a refund of fees paid for the period after termination.

Each subprocessor is bound by a written contract with data protection obligations at least as protective as this agreement. We remain responsible to the Customer for our subprocessors.

7. Transfers outside the EEA

Customer data is stored in the EU, in Frankfurt, Germany. Some subprocessors process data in the United States, as shown on the subprocessors page. Each such transfer is covered by the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, where the subprocessor is certified under it, or by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses (Decision (EU) 2021/914).

8. Helping the Customer

Taking into account the nature of the processing, we help the Customer:

  • answer requests from data subjects exercising their rights. If a data subject contacts us directly, we pass the request to the Customer and do not answer it ourselves unless the Customer asks us to.
  • meet its obligations on security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments and prior consultation with a supervisory authority.

9. Personal data breaches

We notify the Customer without undue delay, and in any case within 48 hours, after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting the Customer's data. We tell the Customer what we know about the breach, its likely consequences and what we are doing about it, and update the Customer as we learn more.

10. When the agreement ends

The Customer can export its data for 30 days after the agreement ends. We then delete the Customer's personal data within 60 days, unless the law requires us to keep it. Copies in our backups are deleted as the backups expire, within a further 30 days, and are not used in the meantime.

11. Audits

We make available all information needed to show that we meet this agreement. The Customer, or an independent auditor bound to confidentiality, may audit our compliance once a year, or after a personal data breach, with 30 days' notice, during working hours and without disrupting our service or other customers' data. Each party bears its own costs. Where a subprocessor's own audit reports or certifications answer the question, we may provide those instead of an on-site audit of that subprocessor.

12. Liability and precedence

Liability under this agreement is governed by the terms. If this agreement and the terms differ on the processing of personal data, this agreement applies. This agreement is governed by Polish law.

Annex: security measures

Access

  • Access to the service by invitation only, with a one-time code sent by email at every sign-in; no passwords
  • An authenticator app required for signing, where the Customer turns it on
  • Roles that limit what each User can see and do
  • Each customer's data kept separate from every other customer's
  • Administrative access to production systems limited to the people who need it, protected by multi-factor authentication

Data

  • Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest (AES-256)
  • Data stored in the EU, in Frankfurt, Germany
  • Encrypted backups

Integrity and records

  • An audit trail of every change (who, when, old and new value), stored append-only, so no past entry can be edited
  • Every signed version frozen exactly as signed
  • Records of who signed each version, when and how they verified it

AI

  • AI suggestions are reviewed by a person before they enter a version for signing
  • Data sent to the AI provider is not used to train its models

Organisation

  • Confidentiality obligations for everyone with access to customer data
  • Subprocessors chosen for their security and bound by data protection terms
  • A process for handling and reporting incidents
